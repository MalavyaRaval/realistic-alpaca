01 Methodology & assumptions

Every one of these is a real modeling choice, stated plainly so the numbers can be judged rather than just trusted.

How a trade is simulated
  • Daily OHLC bars only (no minute/tick data). A stop is "touched" if the day's low reaches it; if the day's open already gaps through it, the fill uses the open (worse), matching how a real stop-turned-market-order behaves on a gap.
  • No lookahead: today's exit check always uses the stop level as ratcheted through prior days only. Today's high can raise tomorrow's stop, never justify today's own exit against a stop that hasn't been set yet.
  • The live strategy is single-shot (one entry, ever). For this backtest, whenever the position goes flat it re-enters next bar — otherwise a 6-year run produces exactly one trade, useless for win rate or profit factor. This measures the stop-management rule, not one specific trade's fate.
  • Full reinvestment each trade (100% in, 100% out); whatever's still open at a period's end is closed at that bar's close for reporting.
Data
  • TSLA daily bars, split-adjusted (TSLA split 5:1 in 2020 and 3:1 in 2022 — unadjusted data would show a fake ~90% "crash" at each split date).
  • Source: Alpaca's free IEX feed, which only carries history back to 2020-07-27 — the real limit on how far back this analysis can reach, not a choice.
  • Sliced into 7 non-overlapping calendar periods (2020 H2 through 2026 YTD) spanning a bull run, a −65% bear year, a sharp recovery, and recent chop — plus the full run for reference.

What this backtest can't tell you
  • One stock, one historical path. The 7 periods below are calendar slices of TSLA's own 2020–2026 history, not independent samples of different market conditions — a config scoring well across all of them shows it survived how TSLA specifically moved, not that it would survive a fundamentally different regime, or transfer to a different stock. No cross-asset validation was run; that would be the single biggest thing to add next (rerunning this same grid on other volatile large-caps and checking whether the same corner of the parameter space still wins).
  • Each sub-period is backtested independently, which means a fresh position is forced open on that period's first bar and force-closed on its last — those calendar-boundary entry/exit prices add their own noise on top of the stop rule being tested.
  • Exposure is 100% for every single configuration shown further down, by construction: this backtest re-enters the instant it goes flat, so capital is deployed essentially the entire time regardless of parameters. That's a property of how this backtest is built to generate enough trades to measure win rate and profit factor at all — it does not describe how the single-shot live strategy actually behaves, which only ever holds one position, once.

02 Robust configurations, not just the highest-return one

Ranked by average Calmar-ratio rank (annualized return ÷ |max drawdown|) across the 5 full calendar years (2021–2025) — a configuration only scores well here if it held up reasonably across most of those years, not just the one that happened to be kindest to it. The two partial-year stretches (2020 H2, 2026 YTD) are deliberately excluded from this score: annualizing a 5-month or partial-year return amplifies small timing noise into large swings, and they'd otherwise carry the same weight as a full year despite covering far less trading time.
