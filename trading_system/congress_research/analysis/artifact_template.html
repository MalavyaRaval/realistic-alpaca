Do the top-ranked traders in Congress stay on top?

Point-in-time performance statistics for 199 members of Congress, computed walk-forward so no ranking ever uses information that hadn't been disclosed yet.

READ FIRST

This report does not claim, and its methodology cannot establish, that any politician used material nonpublic information. A large disclosed excess return is a description of a historical outcome under STOCK Act disclosure, nothing more — it is not evidence of improper conduct, and none is asserted here.

Nothing here implies any politician's past results will continue. The consistency analysis in Section 4 exists specifically to test that assumption, and the result argues against it.

This dataset is disclosure data, not real-time market data — every statistic reflects trades that were already old news by the time they became knowable to an outside observer. See Section 2 for exactly how old.

01 Methodology

Walk-forward, not one lookahead-biased snapshot
  • Five evaluation checkpoints (). At each one, a politician's statistics use ONLY transactions with a disclosure date on or before that checkpoint — not transaction date. A trade that happened before a checkpoint but wasn't yet filed is invisible to that checkpoint's calculation, exactly as it would have been to a real observer.
  • Every price lookup takes the latest available close at or before a reference date, never after — verified directly with unit tests, not just asserted.
Two versions of every return
  • Immediate: priced at the transaction date — the unrealistic best case of having the politician's own timing.
  • Lagged: priced at the disclosure date — the realistic case for anyone trying to copy the trade after actually learning about it. The gap between these two is the direct answer to "does the edge survive the delay."
Data limitations — read before the rankings below
  • Only about 12% of tickers actually traded have price data at all (see the coverage figures above) — price history was fetched for the ~300 most-traded tickers, not the long tail of ~2,300 others. "Rankable" status below is a mechanical proxy for trades large, liquid, well-covered names, not a proxy for skill — it likely under-represents politicians whose activity concentrates in smaller-cap, OTC, or foreign names, which is exactly where a real informational edge (if any existed) might most plausibly show up instead of in mega-caps everyone already prices efficiently.
  • Disclosed amounts are ranges, and most of them are the widest one. STOCK Act filings give a range (e.g. "$1,001–$15,000"), never an exact dollar figure, and every dollar figure in this report is that range's midpoint. About 71% of all disclosed transactions fall in the single lowest bracket ($1,001–$15,000) — a bracket whose ceiling is 15× its floor, far wider (relative to its own floor) than any other bracket. If real amounts cluster toward the floor, as is commonly suspected, the midpoint systematically overstates the size of most trades, which pulls both the dollar-weighted return and the concentration (HHI) figures toward whatever those small, frequent, lowest-bracket trades happened to do.
  • "Portfolio return" is a dollar-weighted average of point-to-point returns, not a time-weighted or annualized return. A 3-week trade and a 3-year holding contribute to it in exact proportion to their disclosed dollar size, never their duration — this is not how a fund's official "return" is normally constructed, and shouldn't be read as one.
  • "Maximum drawdown" is a synthetic index, built by chaining each politician's disclosed trades sequentially in exit-date order and compounding their dollar-weighted returns — not a reconstruction of a real portfolio's actual day-to-day value. Positions this politician genuinely held at the same time get chained one-after-another instead of overlapping, and this politician almost certainly held other assets (salary, funds, real estate) never disclosed here and not 100% invested in just these names. Read this number as "how rough would the ride have looked across these specific disclosed trades," not as this person's real wealth swing.
  • Sector classification is a manually curated mapping for well-known tickers, not a licensed classifier; unmapped tickers are excluded from sector breakdowns rather than guessed.
  • Position matching (which specific shares were sold) uses FIFO (oldest lot first) — a standard, defensible convention, but not something the underlying filings actually specify. In a sustained uptrend, FIFO tends to match a sale against the oldest, cheapest lot, which can inflate the computed gain for politicians who bought the same name repeatedly before selling, versus an average-cost convention.
  • Includes House and Senate members. Senate eFD disclosure data carries a statutory restriction on commercial use beyond news/media dissemination (5 U.S.C. app. §105(c)) — this report is research/analysis, not a trading signal, but that restriction should be kept in mind for any other use of this data.
  • A politician only appears in the ranked tables below with at least 5 transactions, 5 priced legs, and 40% dollar-weighted price coverage — thresholds chosen to keep the ranking from being dominated by tiny, noisy samples, listed here so the cutoff is visible, not hidden.

07 Risks of attempting to copy these transactions

  • The disclosure lag is the whole game. Median reporting delay across rankable politicians is shown in Section 1; by the time a trade is public, price has often already moved. Section 3 shows this isn't hypothetical — several top-immediate performers lose most or all of their apparent edge under the lagged calculation.
  • Small samples produce large, unstable numbers. A politician with 5-10 priced legs can show a triple-digit excess return from one or two large, favorably-timed trades — Section 4's near-zero first-to-last rank correlation is direct evidence that most of this ranking does not persist.
  • Concentration risk is real and disclosed here (HHI). A high-HHI politician's return is dominated by a handful of names; copying them means taking on that same concentration, not a diversified position.
  • This is historical, not investment advice, and not a live feed. Every number is a backward-looking description of disclosed trades that in most cases already closed out or moved on.
  • Execution reality isn't modeled. No commissions, spread, slippage, or tax treatment are included anywhere in this analysis - real copying costs would erode any of these numbers further.
  • A large excess return is not evidence of anything improper. This report does not and cannot establish that any trade involved material nonpublic information; it only describes disclosed, public outcomes under approximate methodology.
